### Problem Statement

During peak lunch hours, college canteens experience significant congestion due to the traditional food ordering and billing process. Students are required to stand in a queue at a common billing counter to select their food, make payments and obtain printed bills. They must then proceed to their respective food counters, wait in another queue and submit the bills to collect their orders.

This sequential process results in increased waiting times, overcrowding and inefficient order distribution. Additionally, food preparation is often delayed because the respective food counters receive order information only when students physically submit their printed bills. The concentration of student arrivals during short lunch breaks further increases the workload on billing and food distribution counters.

The absence of a centralised system for managing orders, monitoring counter workloads and analysing food demand makes it difficult to optimise canteen operations.
